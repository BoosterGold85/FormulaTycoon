# Formula Tycoon Player Manual

This is a living guide to the current prototype. Driver browsing, scouting report reviews,
contract information, calendar/circuit briefings and HQ interactions are available. Racing, a dynamic form engine, sponsors,
payroll and a complete career loop are not available yet. Planned effects are identified below.

## Drivers

Open **DRIVERS** for your current pairing first, then browse Available, Prospects, Reserves or
the full Grid. Compact rows show Talent, form and contract horizon. Tap a driver for the full
dossier and six categories; expand **Contract details**, **Scouting report** or background for
depth. Open **TEAM** for your car's next-circuit suitability, drivers, development and HQ staff.
The starting organization is Ardent Dynamics. No finances or morale are invented for the overview.

Drivers have six broad evaluation categories, shown as letter grades from F to A+:

- **Talent:** speed and qualifying execution.
- **Racecraft:** racing execution and tyre management.
- **Adaptability:** handling unfamiliar cars, changing grip and conditions.
- **Composure:** consistency, discipline and handling pressure.
- **Technical Feedback:** helping an organization understand its car.
- **Marketability:** commercial profile and reach.

There is no universal driver overall rating. A driver can excel in one area and be less effective
in another. Specialties describe additional strengths; some remain unconfirmed until scouted.
Age and career stage provide context, not a promise of scripted future results.

## Driver Performance

The intended race model considers the driver, car, track, conditions, strategy and current form
together. A strong driver in an unsuitable or weak car can have poor results. A young driver can
have an exceptional season without possessing a veteran's technical experience.

The fixed 2026 starting evaluations are a starting snapshot, not a permanent career ceiling.
Adaptability, feedback and commercial value are evaluated separately from current-season pace.
For example, Reyes and Benvenuti retain useful veteran strengths despite weaker starting pace
information. Future careers will be driven by Formula Tycoon outcomes rather than scripted history.

Current form is described as **EXCELLENT**, **GOOD**, **NORMAL**, **POOR** or **STRUGGLING**.
Everyone currently begins at NORMAL. Form does not evolve yet, and no race results are simulated.

## Car Fit

A talented driver may be more comfortable in one kind of car than another. Driver detail shows
an initial **Comfortable**, **Adapting** or **Challenging** estimate for the assigned car, alongside
organization familiarity. An unsigned driver has no assigned car. Familiarity and underlying
skills are separate; changing teams does not erase talent.

The current fit estimate is coarse. Track, weather and detailed handling effects are future work;
this display does not currently apply a race or development bonus.

## Technical Feedback

Experienced, technically strong drivers can have value beyond raw pace. Technical Feedback
represents that organizational contribution: car understanding, setup discussion and useful
engineering information. Veterans such as Quintero, Benvenuti and Reyes can remain valuable here.

Connections to setup, simulator work and upgrade correlation are planned. Feedback does not
currently change development outcomes or generate an additional hidden bonus.

## Marketability

Marketability represents reputation, popularity, nationality and home-country appeal, regional
markets and global reach. These commercial strengths need not match on-track strengths.
Commercial information can be uncertain for incompletely scouted drivers.

Sponsors and commercial payouts are not implemented. A strong commercial grade does not currently
create income.

## Contracts

Driver detail shows annual salary in millions, the guaranteed end season and any existing team
option. Salary is an estimated annual average obligation, not a signing payment. Payroll and
performance-bonus payouts are not simulated yet.

**Guaranteed through** includes that season. **Future guaranteed years after this season** excludes
the current season: at the start of 2026 a deal through 2026 has zero future guaranteed years,
while a deal through 2030 has four. This does not mean the 2026 deal has already expired.

A **team option** is an additional season the team may activate. It is separate from the guaranteed
term until exercised. The profile shows its year, state and decision deadline. Only the owning
team can exercise it, and a conflicting seat or driver booking prevents the action. Pending
options lapse after the displayed deadline. The current Ardent starting pair has no options;
other teams' option terms can be inspected, but their decisions are not yours to make.

After the effective deal ends, the seat becomes vacant unless another contract covers it.
Automatic offseason recruitment, negotiations, buyouts and a full signing screen are future work.

**Confirmed** and **Reported** describe the starting-data source. Both create binding career
contracts with identical mechanical reliability. Newly created career contracts need no such label.
New careers use the aligned 2026 deals; imported earlier prototype careers retain their existing
signed terms and decisions.

A reserve deal is distinct from a race seat. Tane Whitmore is associated with the Moravia/Altair
system and has a Moravia reserve contract through 2026. This reserve is outside the 22-driver starting grid
and does not appear as a third driver in the race-seat TEAM comparison.

## Scouting

Established starting drivers are well known. Prospects and reserve-market drivers may instead
show grade ranges, uncertain potential or **Unknown**. These are estimates, not extra abilities.
True hidden values are not displayed directly.

**Improve scouting report** narrows the uncertainty for your organization without changing the
driver's skills. Other teams keep their own knowledge. Confidence, the last report date and report
count show what you currently know. Report reviews are free and immediate in this prototype;
scouting staff, budgets and elapsed-time assignments are future work.

## Time & Calendar

Your career begins on **Monday, March 2, 2026**. The in-game date stays the same wherever you
play; it is separate from the real date and the clock on your device.

Development and contract deadlines use this shared career calendar. The HQ **NEW DAY** button
currently replays the morning arrivals; it does **not** move the career date forward.

Use **CONTINUE** in the top bar to play a week at a time. It moves the career to the next Monday's
development report, or stops earlier at the first practice of a race weekend. After first practice, the next
step is **PLAN QUALIFYING**, for Saturday. On larger screens, **+1 DAY**
advances a single day. Everything due along the way (development work, fittings, contract deadlines)
happens exactly once, in order. The season calendar can be browsed from RACE without moving time.
In this prototype the development season ends on March 1, 2027; season review is not available yet.

## Calendar & Race Weekends

Open **RACE** for the next-event card and a month calendar. Switch months to browse the
23-round season; tap a marked date, event row or **View weekend** for its briefing. Weekends
occupy their actual dates, including those spanning two months. A **SPRINT** marker identifies a shorter-format
weekend. For now, Sprint weekends run normal Saturday qualifying; Sprint qualifying and Sprint races are not
simulated yet. Once qualifying has run, the weekend's briefing shows the starting grid.

The next event is highlighted. **THIS WEEKEND** includes the opening and race dates.
**COMPLETED** currently means the date has passed; it does not mean a race result was generated.
The HQ next-event card shows the same date-based schedule and countdown.

## Circuit Characteristics

Tap an event for its main demands and fitted-car suitability first. **More details** reveals
all demands and technical background. Circuit illustrations are stylized art, not surveyed maps.
Different tracks reward different technical strengths:
Monza and Las Vegas emphasize straight-line efficiency and braking; Monaco rewards low-speed
performance, traction and mechanical grip; Suzuka and Silverstone emphasize high-speed aero.
Sepang places broad demands on aero, tyres and cooling.

Track demands appear as LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH or VERY HIGH. These are initial game-model descriptions,
not exact measurements. Madrid's current profile reflects its confirmed mixed-speed layout and
strong tyre-energy demands. Imported earlier careers retain their original circuit profiles.

## Car Development

Open **TEAM → Development** to run your factory. **Start a project** lists the eight areas of the car. Each
shows what it helps (for example high-speed aero or traction), upcoming races where that matters, about how
many weeks it takes, its cost and the likely size of the gain.

- **Objective:** Performance, a Reliability fix, or a Track package (a low-drag rear wing or a high-cooling
  bodywork package, which help only at matching circuits).
- **Approach:** Conservative, Balanced or Aggressive. Aggressive work takes longer and costs more. It can bring a
  bigger gain, but it carries more risk and a new part may start out less reliable.
- **Capacity and budget:** how many projects can run at once depends on your Aero and Chassis departments.
  Projects are paid from your season development budget (shown in $M). This is not team finances.

Projects move through **Research → Design → Manufacture → Ready**. Each Monday your departments produce
Research, Aero or Technical, and Manufacturing points; the team works on all active projects at once. Better
facilities and staff produce more points per week. A finished part is fitted at the **next race weekend**,
unless you choose **Hold back** to save it for a later race.

The **HQ** is where you play. Its development card always shows what the factory is building, its stage, overall
progress, the expected ready date and the race weekend it should be fitted at. It also tells you when an engineering
decision is waiting, or when the factory is idle. Press **CONTINUE WEEK** (or **CONTINUE TO FP1** when a race weekend
comes first).

The week is decided the moment you press Continue; the HQ then replays it Monday to Friday. Points rise from the
department doing the work and flow into the project card:

- Aero & Design for wind-tunnel research and aero design;
- the Simulator for rig research;
- the Workshop for chassis design;
- Manufacturing for building the part.

Watch at **1×** or **2×**, or **SKIP** to the summary. The speed never changes what happened. If an engineering
decision comes up during the week, the replay pauses and asks you straight away. The week's results are already decided,
so your answer applies to the project from then on. You can watch the rest of the week first, but the game won't advance
until you decide. While a decision is open, the Continue button reads **DECISION REQUIRED** and the HQ card shows the
options. Ordinary good and bad weeks never pause the game. When a week moves the ready date or the race a part
will be fitted at, the card shows the new and old estimates side by side. After the replay the HQ card keeps a short
**Last week** note.

Not every week goes to plan:

- **Most weeks:** the team simply delivers.
- **Setback or stall:** a department may be slowed by a setback, or stall completely (a correlation problem, rig
  failure, design rework or prototype failure). Its bubbles dry up, a warning appears over it, and the ready date can
  slip, sometimes past the race you were aiming for. Serious problems in design or manufacturing can also weaken the
  part.
- **Breakthrough:** a department may find a way to work much faster, or find extra performance that makes the part
  better than first predicted.
- **Your facilities and staff matter:** better ones stall less often and break through more often.

Reports where parts were fitted show what changed on the car and at that weekend's circuit, with a short reason for the
result. The **Paddock** section mentions what rival teams brought, and occasionally a rumour about their development.

Development rarely goes exactly to plan. A project may hit a **wind tunnel correlation issue**, a
**prototype defect** or a **material shortage**, spot a **rival's concept**, be offered **extra simulation
time** or a **lighter material**, or produce a **breakthrough**. You choose how to respond,
and the game waits for your answer before it moves on. Investigating or remaking costs time or money but can
make the result more dependable. **Refine design** spends extra time and money for a better part.

When a part is fitted, the result is shown as **ABOVE EXPECTATION**, **ON TARGET**, **BELOW EXPECTATION**
or **DIDN'T WORK — REVERTED** (money spent, previous part kept). Your car's suitability at each circuit
updates from the parts actually fitted. Rival teams develop under the same rules. Careers imported from
earlier versions keep their original development rules and fitting schedule. Careers started before this update do not
get weekly setbacks and breakthroughs.

## Car Characteristics

The technical model reads the parts fitted to your car. Aero components, suspension, cooling,
power-unit performance and reliability feed different characteristics. A project still in the
factory does not improve the fitted car. Reverted upgrades do not create a hidden improvement.
Existing low-drag and cooling packages apply only where their technical conditions match.

## Car / Track Fit

Circuit briefings show your team's **EXCELLENT**, **STRONG**, **COMPETITIVE**, **MIXED**, **WEAK**
or **POOR** technical suitability, with relevant strengths and concerns. A single universal car
rating cannot describe every circuit. Upgrades may help more at some venues than others.

A hot venue can demand strong cooling without unusually high mechanical stress or tyre wear.
Cold tyre warm-up and tyre wear are different challenges; detailed warm-up is not simulated yet.

Technical suitability is only one part of eventual race performance. It excludes driver ability,
form, strategy and dynamic weather, and predicts no finishing position. It is separate from the
driver's comfort with the car shown in Driver Detail. Qualifying uses your fitted car at the circuit;
races remain future work.

## Qualifying

Every race weekend has a Saturday qualifying session. Your two drivers fight the other 20 cars for grid positions.

**Format.** The session is a knockout:
- **Q1** (18 minutes): all 22 cars run; the slowest 6 are knocked out (P17–P22).
- **Q2** (15 minutes): 16 cars run; another 6 are knocked out (P11–P16).
- **Q3** (12 minutes): the top 10 decide pole and the first five rows.

**Your plan.** After first practice, **PLAN QUALIFYING** opens the plan screen, which shows Saturday's forecast
and how well your car suits the circuit. For each driver and each segment, choose:
- **Tyres:** a new soft is quickest. A used soft or the medium can save a new set for later. Inters are for a wet track.
- **Runs:** one or two runs. A second run is another chance, but it uses another set and spends more time in traffic.
- **Go out:** early, middle or late. Early means a clean track with less grip, and it's safe if rain is coming. Late
  means the most grip, but busier traffic and more weather risk.
- **Traffic:** find space or push through. Finding space waits for a gap; very late in a segment, that can cost you
  the lap. Pushing through is quicker to start, but you more often hit traffic.

Each car has 4 new soft sets and 1 medium set for the whole session. The team's strategists recommend a plan;
you can keep it or change it. Lock the plan to start qualifying.

**Weather.** A session can stay dry, have rain arrive, or start wet and dry out. The forecast can be wrong. Its
confidence (Low, Medium or High) improves with the Research work your factory has done this season. That is the
same Research shown as bubbles in the week replay. Even a well-researched forecast is never certain.

**Live session and calls.** The session is replayed on a broadcast-style timing screen, sector by sector. The
header shows a **LIVE** dot (PAUSED while the pit wall waits for you, FINAL at the flag), which part is running
(Q1, Q2, Q3) and the time left in it.
- **Your cars** shows each driver's out lap, flying lap and in lap. As the lap runs, each sector shows its gap to the
  fastest time in that sector, plus where the lap would put the driver at its pace so far ("On pace for P9").
- **Sector colours:** **purple** is fastest of anyone so far; **green** is that driver's personal best; **yellow** is
  slower than their best.
- **The tower:** each row has the team's colour bar and a three-letter driver code; tap a row (or hover on a
  computer) for the full name and every figure. Columns show the gap to P1, the gap to the car ahead, the last lap
  against that driver's own best (**PB**, a time slower, or **OFF** for a lost lap), the three sectors, the tyre
  (**S** soft, **M** medium, **I** intermediate; a dashed ring is a used set) and an arrow when a car has gained or
  lost places in the last minute. Phones show the essentials. Cars on a flying lap are at full strength; cars on
  out laps, in laps or in the garage are greyed. Your cars always have a ring in your team's colour. A red line marks the **drop zone**, and rivals fighting around it pulse while
  they're on a flying lap. Rows slide as positions change.
- **Mini-sectors:** each sector is a row of small blocks that fill in as the car drives through it, then all take
  the sector's colour once the sector time is in. They show progress only, never their own timing.
- **Track map** (Melbourne so far): a simplified circuit outline with the sector boundaries, a dot for every car
  out on track in its team colour, and your cars ringed in white with their code. It shows where each car is in
  the replay; it is a stylized picture, not a measured layout.
- **Focus** shows only your cars and the battle around the drop zone (in Q3, the fight for pole).
- **Commentary** covers the whole field ("Holub goes fastest!"). **Team radio**, with your team's badge, is
  your engineer talking to your drivers ("Good S1. You're up two tenths on your best.").
- **Pace:** the replay lingers when something happens and moves faster when nothing does. You can watch at 1×, 2×
  or 4×; Skip jumps to the next stop.

On a wide screen, the timing tower sits on the left and the pit wall on the right, so you see the field while you
decide. On a phone, the call opens as a sheet over the tower; tap its header to fold it away. Cars without a lap
time have no position yet and are listed under "No time yet". A row flashes briefly when that car finishes a lap.
**+1 DAY** is locked while a session is live.

Halfway through each part the pit wall asks for one call per driver: stick to the plan, go now, wait for the end,
or stay in the garage to save tyres. Each option says what it costs, and the call shows the pace currently needed to
get through (early in a part, the previous part's pace). On a wet or drying track you can also change tyres. A recommendation is
marked; you can always choose differently.

**On-track events.** The call can come from something happening on track instead of the halfway point:
- **Conditions change:** when rain starts to fall or the track starts to dry, the pit wall asks whether to go out
  now or wait.
- **Yellow flag:** when race control reports debris, cars heading out now will meet a yellow flag in that sector.
  For each of your cars on that lap, choose:
  - **Abort the lap:** it's lost, but the driver can go again a couple of minutes later if time allows. That uses
    another set of tyres.
  - **Push through:** keeps the lap, but the driver loses time lifting, and the lap can be deleted for not slowing
    enough.

- **Red flag:** the session stops and the clock pauses. Any car on a flying lap loses that lap. For the restart,
  choose:
  - **Go out first:** head the queue with the most time left, but the whole field leaves with you.
  - **Wait for a clean track:** leave two minutes later with less time to spare.
  - **Wait for the end**, or **stay in.**

If nothing happens in a part, the call comes at the halfway point as usual, so every part has one decision.
Commentary and team radio cover these events too.

**Breaks.** The session stops after Q1 and after Q2. The break shows who was knocked out, whether your cars made
it, and the order going into the next part. If you have a car still running, you can change its plan for the next
part there. Nothing resumes until you press Continue; Skip and the speed controls stop there too. The replay only
shows what your team has already run: speed, Skip and Focus never change it.

**Result.** Afterwards, each of your drivers gets a result card: helmet in team colours, a big grid position and a
headline for the story of the session ("0.02s from Q2", "Knocked out by teammate Lund", "Front row"). Below that is
the full starting grid in team colours, with lines where Q1 and Q2 knocked cars out. Each driver's position also comes
with a short "why": how close the knockout line
was, traffic, mistakes, tyre choices, the weather call, and how well the car suits the circuit. The grid is saved
with the weekend and appears in **RACE** in that weekend's briefing. Your car's performance matters most; your
choices, the driver and the weather decide the rest. The race itself is not simulated yet. Rival teams make their
own plans and calls.

## Headquarters and saving

Use **HQ** to return to the working headquarters. Tap workers or departments for their cards;
**NEW DAY** restarts the workday. Navigation keeps the same live game session.

The tested save format preserves driver details, contracts and scouting information, including
older-format migration. A player-facing save/load screen and durable device storage do not exist
yet. Closing or reloading the prototype should not be treated as saving a career.

## Roster packs

The game ships with its own fictional grid: 11 original teams, 22 race drivers and 21 free
agents. Open **SETTINGS** in the top bar to change who you see.

- **Choose pack file** (web) or paste the pack's text, and the game checks it. If anything is
  wrong you get a list of problems, each naming the team or driver slot and what to fix. Nothing
  changes until a pack passes.
- A passing pack shows a **preview** of every team with its colours and drivers, numbers,
  nationalities and helmets. Entries the pack leaves out use the default roster, marked DEFAULT.
- **Cosmetic packs** change names, colours, car numbers, nationality labels, bios and helmets.
  Apply them to your current career at any time: results, stats and your save are unchanged.
- **Full packs** can also change driver stats (age, ratings, potential, traits, home market).
  Stats only take effect when you **start a new career** with the pack, which ends the current
  one; they never change a season in progress. You can also apply only a full pack's names and
  colours to your current career.
- **Reset to default roster** brings back the default names and colours. A career started from a
  full pack keeps its stats; start a new career to return to the default stats too.
- **Download the default pack** (web) gives you a complete pack to edit.

Packs map onto the grid by slot: team slot 6 is always your organization, and driver slots 11
and 12 are its race drivers. On web the game remembers your pack between visits; a remembered
full pack is used for each new career. Pack authors: see `docs/ROSTER_PACKS.md` in the project.

## Appearance

The default presentation uses warm light content inside a dark racing shell. Use **DARK** in the
top bar for dark content, and **LIGHT** to switch back. Web remembers this preference
locally when browser storage is available. This remembers appearance only: it does not save your
campaign. Switching themes preserves the current session; native persistent appearance settings
are not implemented yet. Team colors stay the same in both modes.
